World map — renewable share of electricity, 2024 (%)Fixed comparison — ignores slicers by design

0%100% renewable No data / not on simplified map
Stat: 20 of 195 countries ran on ≥ 90% renewable electricity in 2024; seven (Albania, Bhutan, DR Congo, Ethiopia, Iceland, Nepal, Paraguay) reached 100%. Takeaway: small hydro states dominate the global list — Germany is #55 worldwide but #5 among the 36 large producers (≥ 100 TWh). 27 small island states in the data are not drawn on this simplified map.

Heatmap — renewable share (%) of the 12 largest producersFixed comparison — ignores slicers by design

Stat: Germany improved most of the twelve: 6.2% → 58.6% (+52.4 pp, 2000→2024). Brazil stays greenest (87.3%), Saudi Arabia lowest (2.2%). Takeaway: the transition is fastest where policy pushed wind and solar.

Slope chart — renewable share 2000 → 2024Fixed comparison — ignores slicers by design

Stat: Denmark climbed steepest (+73.7 pp); France gained least (+14.4 pp). Poland started last of the four but overtook France by 2024 — the only rank swap. Takeaway: Denmark, not Germany, is the fastest mover of the four.

Waterfall — Germany generation bridge 2000 → 2024 (TWh)Fixed comparison — ignores slicers by design

Stat: the bridge closes exactly — 569.0 + (−73.0) = 496.0 TWh. Coal (−190) and the nuclear exit (−170) outweigh wind (+132), solar (+74), bioenergy (+47) and gas (+29). Takeaway: renewables replaced most, but not all, of the fossil/nuclear exit, so Germany generates less electricity overall than in 2000. Bioenergy is split out of “Other renewables” and oil is included, per the raw OWID source columns.

Waffle — Germany's 2024 electricity mix (1 square = 1%)Fixed comparison — ignores slicers by design

Stat: renewables hold 59 of the 100 squares vs the exact 58.6% (largest-remainder rounding); wind alone is 29 squares. Takeaway: wind is the single biggest slice of the mix; nuclear holds none.
